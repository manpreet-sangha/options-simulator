📈 Long Straddle Strategy Volatility Bet
A Long Straddle involves buying a call AND buying a put at the same strike price (K) and expiration. This strategy profits when the stock makes a large move in either direction. You're essentially betting on high volatility — you don't care which way the stock moves, as long as it moves significantly!

Cost: You pay both premiums upfront (C₀ + P₀), making this an expensive strategy. The stock must move enough to cover this cost for you to profit.

📐 Profit Formulas
If ST > K (stock rises):
Profit = (ST − K) − (C₀ + P₀)
If ST < K (stock falls):
Profit = (K − ST) − (C₀ + P₀)
Lower Breakeven:
SBE = K − (C₀ + P₀)
Upper Breakeven:
SBE = K + (C₀ + P₀)
📉 Short Straddle Strategy Neutral / Low Volatility
A Short Straddle involves selling a call AND selling a put at the same strike price (K) and expiration. This strategy profits when the stock stays near the strike price and doesn't move much. You're betting on low volatility — you collect premiums upfront and hope the options expire worthless or near-worthless.

⚠️ Warning: This is a high-risk strategy with unlimited loss potential if the stock makes a large move in either direction!

🎯 When to Use Long Straddle
📰
Before Earnings Announcements
Companies often have big stock moves after earnings. A long straddle profits whether the news is good or bad!
⚖️
FDA Drug Approvals
Biotech stocks can soar or crash based on FDA decisions. Perfect for straddles when you expect a binary outcome.
🏛️
Major Economic Events
Federal Reserve announcements, elections, or geopolitical events can cause significant market volatility.
📊
Low Implied Volatility
When options are "cheap" (low IV) but you expect a big move, long straddles can be particularly profitable.
⚠️ Real World Cautionary Tale: Nick Leeson & Barings Bank
Nick Leeson, a trader at Barings Bank, sold thousands of short straddles on the Nikkei-225 index, betting that the Japanese market would remain stable. He initially received premiums of C₀ + P₀ = 8 (per contract).

However, the 1995 Kobe earthquake caused the Nikkei-225 to plummet dramatically. Since short straddles have unlimited downside risk, Leeson's losses spiraled out of control, eventually reaching £827 million — enough to bankrupt Barings Bank, one of the oldest merchant banks in the UK!

Lesson: Short straddles can generate steady income in calm markets, but catastrophic losses during unexpected events. Always understand your maximum risk exposure!
